03 METHOD & LIMITS

A dispatch rule you can inspect.

One hourly step at a time, we route only coincident solar surplus into storage and only discharge against a coincident demand deficit.

A / INPUT

Scale the solar profile

NESO’s estimated embedded solar profile is multiplied by a single factor so annual solar energy equals the selected share of annual National Demand.

solar′t = solart × (share × Σ demand / Σ solar)
B / CHARGE

Use the surplus

Charging is limited by coincident surplus, the charge-power limit, and the space left below maximum state of charge.

charget = min(surplus, Pc, (SOCmax − SOCt) / ηc)
C / DISCHARGE

Meet the deficit

Discharge is limited by the hourly deficit, the discharge-power limit, and usable stored energy above minimum state of charge.

discharget = min(deficit, Pd, (SOCt − SOCmin) × ηd)
D / ACCOUNT

Keep each flow visible

Uncaptured surplus is curtailed; demand not served by direct solar or the battery remains residual demand met by other sources. Conversion losses are tracked.

SOCt+1 = SOCt + ηc × charge − discharge / ηd

WHAT THIS MODEL CAN SAY

It can compare this rule’s hourly energy flows under explicit capacity, power, efficiency and solar-share assumptions. One-hour average MW is numerically equal to MWh for that interval.

WHAT IT CANNOT SAY

This is an unconstrained GB-wide energy balance. It does not represent location, transmission congestion, degradation, reserve obligations, grid charging, price formation, project cost or reliability. A national surplus is not necessarily available to a battery at one site.

05 ABOUT THIS PROJECT

A small instrument for a large system.

BatteryWise is an open-source project by a final-year computer science student, built to make grid-storage assumptions visible and reproducible. It asks a narrow question: when does storage shift solar into hours when demand needs it?

The simulator is a learning and analysis tool—not an investment recommendation, grid forecast, or claim that a single battery can operate at national scale. The Python reference model and browser model share the same golden dispatch example.

06 OPEN ROADMAP

Useful next steps.

Small, scoped contributions that extend the analysis without hiding its assumptions.

  1. DATAAdd a second licensed grid dataset

    Choose a country with a complete year of demand and generation, document the licence, reconcile time conventions, and add a validation report.

  2. DISPATCHCompare a different dispatch rule

    Add a strategy interface and compare greedy surplus-first dispatch with a clearly documented alternative.

  3. MODELTrack renewable origin in state of charge

    Separate solar-origin energy from any configured initial state of charge so solar-served metrics remain precise.

  4. OUTPUTAdd a reproducible scenario JSON export

    Include source version, assumptions, units, aggregate results and a hash of the processed input series.

  5. ACCESSImprove chart keyboard inspection

    Add keyboard-accessible hourly point inspection and a compact tabular alternative for every chart.

  6. VALIDATIONAdd conservation checks for the full year

    Validate interval and annual energy balances for multiple efficiencies, state-of-charge bounds and battery sizes.
